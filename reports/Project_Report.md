*Written so that someone new to data analytics can follow every step. Technical terms are explained when they first appear.*

## 1. Introduction

Businesses record every sale they make. On its own, a list of thousands of sales says nothing. Data analysis turns that list into answers: *Where do we make money? Where do we lose it? What should we do?*

This project analyses four years of sales at **RetailCo**, a fictional US retailer of furniture, office supplies and technology. It goes all the way from a messy raw export to an interactive Power BI dashboard and a set of business recommendations. Python, SQL, Power BI and DAX are each used for the job they do best.

## 2. Business problem

RetailCo's sales grew in 2024, but its profit fell, and leadership could not explain why. The existing reports showed only revenue. The Head of Sales asked for:

1. A true picture of each region's performance, including **profit**, not just sales.
2. Where the company is **losing money**.
3. A **dashboard** they can keep using.
4. **Recommendations** for the leadership meeting.

(Full scenario and stakeholders: [docs/company_scenario.md](../docs/company_scenario.md).)

## 4. Dataset description

* **Source:** `data/raw/sales_raw.csv`. It is synthetic, generated by `src/generate_data.py` with a fixed seed, using the same layout as the public *Superstore* dataset.
* **Size:** 11,567 rows × 19 columns (raw); 11,461 rows × 32 columns (clean).
* **Period:** 1 Jan 2021 – 31 Dec 2024.
* **Grain:** one row = one product on one order (an "order line"). One order can have 1–5 lines.
* **Why synthetic?** It can be shared freely. It contains realistic, *known* errors to clean. And because the error-free version exists, the cleaning can be **tested** against the truth. The patterns come from openly documented business rules: regional growth rates, discount policies by region, product margins and seasonality.

## 5. Data dictionary

The full dictionary (type, meaning, range, missing/duplicate rules, and whether each column can be aggregated) is in [docs/data_dictionary.md](../docs/data_dictionary.md). The essentials:

| Kind | Columns |
|---|---|
| Identifiers | Order ID, Customer ID, Product ID |
| Dates | Order Date, Ship Date |
| Descriptions (categorical) | Ship Mode, Customer Name, Segment, Country, City, State, Region, Category, Sub-Category, Product Name |
| Measures (numeric) | Sales, Quantity, Discount, Profit |

**Sales** is revenue after discount. **Profit** is sales minus the cost of goods and can be negative. **Discount** is a fraction (0.2 = 20%).

## 6. Data cleaning

**Principles:** fix before you delete; delete only what can't be trusted *and* can't be rebuilt; never invent money; unusual ≠ wrong; log everything.

| Step | Problem found | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strip whitespace | `"  Boston "` | Trim | Spaces at the edges carry no meaning |
| Region | 35 blank; variants like `east`, `Centrl` | Rebuild from State | Each state belongs to exactly one region (verified) |
| Category | `Tech`, `FURNITURE` | Re-derive from Sub-Category | Each sub-category belongs to one category |
| Sales type | `"$1,277.10"` stored as text | Remove symbols, convert | Real sales, badly formatted |
| Duplicates | 70 identical rows | Remove copies | Would double-count revenue. Repeated **Order IDs are kept**: they are multi-product orders |
| Discount | `20` instead of `0.2` | ÷100 when the result is a valid level | Intent is unambiguous |
| Names | 45 missing | Look up via ID | The ID is complete and reliable |
| Order Date | `2023-02-30`, `not recorded` | Remove (5) | Can't place the sale in time |
| Ship Date | Before Order Date | Blank the ship date, keep the row (6) | The sale is real; only shipping data is wrong |
| Sales/Profit missing | 17 rows | Remove | Can't be rebuilt; imputing would invent money |
| Sales ≤ 0, Quantity ≤ 0 | 14 rows | Remove | Impossible for a sale. **Negative profit is kept** (real losses) |
| Outliers | 1,016 lines above the IQR fence | **Flag, keep** | They are genuine expensive products: 42% of revenue |

**Result:** 11,567 → **11,461 rows (99.1% kept)**. 275 values were repaired rather than deleted.
**Proof of correctness:** `tests/test_pipeline.py` compares the cleaned data with the generator's error-free data. Every repaired value matches the truth, no row was invented, and total sales and profit are within 1% of the truth.

## 7. Feature engineering

| Feature | Formula | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Year, Quarter, Month, Month Number, Year-Month | from Order Date | Time grouping |
| Shipping Days | Ship Date − Order Date | Delivery speed |
| Profit Margin % | Profit / Sales × 100 | Row-level filter |
| Unit Price | Sales / Quantity | Price paid per unit |
| Gross Sales | Sales / (1 − Discount) | Value at list price |
| Discount Amount | Gross Sales − Sales | Revenue given away |
| Is Loss, Discount Band | rules | Filters and readable groups |

**Why Profit Margin matters:** profit alone favours big regions. Margin (profit per $1 of sales) compares regions of different sizes fairly. **A group's margin is always SUM(Profit)/SUM(Sales)**; averaging row-level margins gives 11.7% instead of the correct 10.3%.

## 8. Exploratory data analysis

Full report with charts: [EDA_Report.md](EDA_Report.md). The main observations:

* West has the highest sales and profit; East the highest margin (13.2%); Central the lowest (3.3%).
* 2024: sales +6.7%, profit −6.6%.
* South's sales are falling 12% per year; West and East are growing 12–14% per year.
* Strong seasonality: Sep, Nov and Dec are the peaks; Q4 is 37% of the year.
* Tables and Bookcases lose money in every region; Furniture is break-even.
* Discounts of 30%+ are loss-making at every level; correlation between discount and margin is −0.81.
* The top 20% of customers produce 54% of sales.

Every chart was chosen for its question. Bars compare categories; lines show time; a scatter shows two measures per product; a heatmap shows a two-dimensional matrix. There are no dual axes, and no pie charts for close values.

| KPI | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Total Sales | SUM(Sales) | $4,391,176 |
| Total Profit | SUM(Profit) | $452,068 |
| Profit Margin | SUM(Profit)/SUM(Sales) | 10.3% |
| Total Orders | COUNT DISTINCT(Order ID) | 5,190 |
| Total Quantity | SUM(Quantity) | 56,703 |
| Average Order Value | Sales / Orders | $846 |
| YoY Sales Growth (2024) | (2024 − 2023) / 2023 | +6.7% |
| YoY Profit Growth (2024) | same, on profit | −6.6% |
| Effective Discount | SUM(Discount Amount)/SUM(Gross Sales) | 15.5% |
| Top Region / Category | highest sales | West / Technology |

Definitions, business meaning, importance and limitations of each KPI are in notebook 04, section 1.
**Key distinctions:** *Sales = Revenue* (after discount) ≠ *Gross Sales* (list price) ≠ *Profit* (after cost) ≠ *Margin* (profit as a %). *Orders* (distinct IDs) ≠ *order lines* (rows) ≠ *Quantity* (units).

## 10. Power BI data model

A **star schema**: one fact table (`FactSales`, one row per order line, holding the numbers) and four dimension tables (`DimDate`, `DimCustomer`, `DimProduct`, `DimGeography`, holding the descriptions). The relationships are one-to-many with single-direction filters (dimension → fact). The same model exists in MySQL with primary-key, foreign-key and check constraints.

**Why:** each description is stored once; filters behave predictably; time intelligence needs a proper date table; new facts (targets, returns) can share the same dimensions. Details and Power Query steps: [powerbi/Data_Model.md](../powerbi/Data_Model.md).

## 13. Business insights

Written as **fact → possible explanation → what to investigate → action** in [Business_Insights.md](Business_Insights.md):

1. 2024 growth was not profitable.
2. Central's low margin is associated with discounting, not with its product mix.
3. Tables and Bookcases lose money everywhere.
4. Deep discounts (30%+) are consistently loss-making.
5. South is shrinking but still profitable.
6. West and East drive all of the growth.
7. Revenue is concentrated in the top customers.

## 14. Recommendations

1. Require approval for discounts ≥ 30%, piloted in Central.
2. Re-price or renegotiate costs for Tables and Bookcases.
3. Include margin in sales targets and incentives.
4. Run a customer-retention review in the South.
5. Prepare West/East capacity for the Sep–Dec peak.
6. Run a margin-based key-account programme for the top 20% of customers.

**Sizing:** up to $264K extra profit if 30%+ discounts had been 20% with unchanged volume. This is an upper bound, not a forecast.

## 15. Limitations

* **Synthetic data:** findings show the method; the patterns come from documented generator rules.
* **Costs:** only cost of goods is included. Shipping, returns and overheads are missing, so true losses may be larger.
* **Causation:** the data shows associations. Why Central discounts more can't be answered from transactions alone.
* **Price elasticity:** the what-if assumes unchanged volume.
* **No targets or budget data**, so performance is measured against other regions and past years, not against plans.
* The `.pbix` file has to be built manually in Power BI Desktop (Windows); the repository provides the complete specification.

## 16. Future improvements

## 17. Conclusion

The project shows the full analyst workflow on a realistic problem. Messy data was cleaned with documented, tested decisions. The analysis went beyond "who sells the most" to "who makes money, and why not". The data was modelled the way BI teams do it, and the results were turned into a dashboard and actions for specific owners. The headline is a lesson every business learns eventually: **sales growth is not the same as profit growth.** At RetailCo, deep discounting, concentrated in one region and one product group, quietly consumed the gains.
